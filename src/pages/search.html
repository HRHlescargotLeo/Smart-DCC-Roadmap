What we're proposing

A site search that understands the words people actually type, puts pages ahead of the 1,494 documents when the question is a plain one, answers acronyms with a definition, and sends members of the public to the help that fits them. Duplicates go, and archived items only appear when asked for.

Why

Search is where people go when the navigation fails them, and on the live site it often fails them too. "My smart meter is not working" returns no results. "Onboarding" returns a redacted consultancy contract first, and a 2020 article about starting a job in lockdown appears twice. The index holds 2,152 items, which is plenty to find something useful if the ranking lets it.

The index here holds the pages in this pack, 35 documents, the consultations and a few news items. Ranking and synonyms are a working sketch of the rules we'd propose, not a finished search engine.

Try:

BehaviourA best bet appears above the results for two kinds of search: an acronym or term in the glossary, which gets its definition, and a question about someone's own meter, bill or display, which gets the public help page. Editors can pin other best bets in the CMS.

    RankingTitle matches count most, pages rank above documents for the same match, archived items rank lowest and are hidden by default, and the same item never appears twice. Common words such as "my", "is" and "the" are ignored. Words people use ("charges", "join", "broken") are matched to the words the site uses ("charging", "onboarding", "public").
